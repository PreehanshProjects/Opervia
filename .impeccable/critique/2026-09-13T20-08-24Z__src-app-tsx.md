## Design Specificity Verdict

Split verdict, and the split IS the finding: the printed sheet is authored for a Mauritian paper invoice book; the screen is a category-interchangeable SaaS dashboard wrapped around it.

InvoicePrint.tsx: "CASH / CREDIT SALES" title block, dotted leader lines sized for a pen, exactly fourteen blank rows, two signature lines, BRN on both parties, en-MU MUR formatting, cell borders drawn only in the print stylesheet (styles.css:2121-2128).

The screen: fixed 246px rail, workspace chip with initial-letter avatar and green status dot, WORKSPACE nav label, "Workspace > Overview" breadcrumb, connection pill, circular avatar, four KPI cards, main panel plus right rail with promo card. Nothing in the shell knows it is replacing a book.

## Overall Impression

A genuinely specific document engine surrounded by a generic app. The peak — the modal swapping from form to rendered A4 sheet with the user's own trading name and signature lines — is correctly placed at the end of the primary task. The worst things are two silent data-destruction bugs on the first-time user's path.

Biggest opportunity: the carbon copy. The paper book's real feature was last week's sheet sitting under this week's blank one. Opervia replaces it with an empty grid, so for repeat business the app is measurably slower than what it replaces.

## What's Working

1. **The print surface is authored, not exported.** table-header-group so multi-page invoices repeat headers; break-inside: avoid on rows/totals/signatures; print-color-adjust: exact; fourteen blank rows; handwriting-grid borders only on paper.
2. **Financial honesty in language.** "Net cash movement / Payments received minus expenses." "Invoices less payments · expenses shown separately." "No ledger entry is created." Names the boundary of its claim.
3. **A real accessibility floor in ModalShell (:87-120).** Focus trap with shift-Tab wrap, initial focus, focus restoration, Escape, role=dialog + aria-modal, scroll lock, per-field aria-labels, prefers-reduced-motion. Status is never color-alone — domain.ts:136 returns literal strings, all three render sites print them. SC 1.4.1 passes.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] "Add customer" from inside the invoice form destroys the invoice in progress

**What:** onAddCustomer={() => setModal("customer")} (App.tsx:1203). The invoice modal renders under modal === "invoice" (:1070), so this unmounts InvoiceForm and destroys its useState. Verified in source.

**Why it matters:** Reached from the setup-note button that renders precisely when !data.customers.length — the first-time user's path. Silent, total data loss on the primary task.

**Fix:** Make modal a stack so the customer dialog renders above the invoice dialog; on save set the returned customer as input.customer_id and pop back. Interim: lift InvoiceForm's input state into App.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

### [P0] Enter in any item field posts the invoice to the ledger

**What:** The invoice form (:1511) contains a submit button with no explicit type (:1774), so it is type="submit". Verified in source.

**Why it matters:** Enter-to-next-row is the reflex of anyone entering rows quickly. The consequence is an immutable, void-only financial record created before the second line was finished.

**Fix:** Intercept Enter in item inputs to append a row and focus its Description field; restrict submit to explicit click or Cmd/Ctrl+Enter.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

### [P1] Session expiry destroys work, and the app speaks Postgres

**What:** SIGNED_OUT (:197-202) wipes data, clears selected, force-closes the modal, drops the user on Auth where the first thing they read is "Good to have you here." errorText (:228) surfaces raw Supabase messages. Write failures have no retry.

**Why it matters:** The moment that decides whether an owner trusts Opervia. Wastes the best safety property — input.id is a client UUID and the RPC is idempotent, so retrying is provably safe and the user is never told.

**Fix:** Retain the draft on SIGNED_OUT and render "Your session ended — sign in to finish this invoice." Map real Postgres errors to plain sentences. Put "Try again" beside every write error with "It is safe to try again — this invoice will not be created twice."

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

### [P1] Accessibility: 30 of 49 contrast pairs fail, including all six status badges

**What:** Computed sRGB ratios. Zero of six badges pass at 10px: void 3.12, partial 3.19, neutral 3.24, overdue 3.47, unpaid 3.50, paid 4.35 (need 4.5:1). Input focus ring is 2.07:1 (#9fbd90 on white, styles.css:104) against a 3:1 requirement — the sole focus affordance for every text field. .data-table th is 2.67:1 at 9px. The --muted token itself is 3.58:1.

**Why it matters:** The badges are the scanning mechanism of a receivables ledger. They pass color-alone but fail legibility — at a counter, on a phone, often by someone over forty. DESIGN.md commits to "at least as legible as the sheet it replaces."

**Fix:** Darken every status foreground to clear 4.5:1 at 10px, or raise badges to 11-12px. Replace the focus ring with the #557d4b already used for buttons. Floor secondary text at #6b7566. Raise table body to 13px, th to 11px.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable audit, then /impeccable typeset

### [P1] Mobile is nominally primary and mechanically secondary

**What:** The 390px viewport (iPhone 12-15 baseline) falls in the gap between the 760px and 380px breakpoints. At 390px .form-grid, .customer-grid and .right-stack all stay multi-column (single-column rules only at max-width 380px, styles.css:2046-2054). Eleven interactive target classes under 44x44: .icon-button is 34x34 and serves as mobile nav opener, modal close, row action and item-delete; the invoice row action shrinks to 24x34 on mobile; .text-button ~15px; .demo-banner button ~12px. .invoice-paper min-width 640px in a 390px window means ~39% of the deliverable is off-screen. The modal has no max-height, non-sticky header, inline footer — on the invoice form both title and Save scroll out of view.

**Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md says phone and desktop are equal surfaces. Mechanically they are not.

**Fix:** Move the small breakpoint from 380px to 420px. Raise .icon-button to 44x44 (shrink the glyph, not the target). Give .modal a max-height with sticky header and sticky action bar. Scale the invoice preview to fit width on mobile.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable adapt

### [P1] Re-entry cost: it is not faster than a pen

**What:** No duplicate-invoice action. No memory of last prices, units or sections. Unsearchable customer select. Number fields snap to 0 when cleared. No paste handling for a column of lines.

**Why it matters:** For the repeat-business case the paper book let you copy; Opervia makes you compose, every time.

**Fix:** "Invoice again" cloning items into a fresh draft with today's dates. Filterable customer combobox. Suggest last price for a matching description. Hold number inputs as strings.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable optimize

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (accessibility-dependent):** Focus rings clipped — outline-offset: 4px with .panel { overflow: hidden } and .table-scroll { overflow-x: auto } cuts the ring on row chevrons. Inputs use :focus not :focus-visible, so the 2.07:1 ring fires on mouse click. nav button.active has no aria-current="page". The six filter chips have no role=tablist/aria-selected. The payment disclosure has no aria-expanded and never moves focus. Every dialog opens focused on "Close" — ModalShell picks the first button in DOM order. prefers-color-scheme entirely absent.

**Casey (one-handed mobile):** "New invoice" lives in the page heading and scrolls away; no FAB, no sticky bar. Interruption is unrecoverable — a call, app switch or stray Escape destroys the invoice with no warning. Backspace in Unit price yields 0, so every correction is select-all-replace. The deliverable needs two-finger horizontal scrolling.

**Riley (stress tester):** loadData fetches all invoices, payments, customers and expenses up to 100,000 rows — and sync() re-runs it after every write. Rows render unvirtualised, calling status() and balance() three times each, every one scanning the entire payments array — O(rows x payments) on every keystroke in an undebounced search box. Excel paste drops the whole blob into one field. Double-submit is safe (idempotent RPC) but shows nothing during a slow write.

**Priya (the helper sharing the owner's login):** Sign-out is a 34px unlabelled icon at the bottom of the sidebar, behind a hamburger on mobile, with persistSession: true and no idle lock. She is never told whose account she is in — the email appears only in the Settings security card. Copy tells her the book is hers when it isn't: "Private workspace", "Your business, in balance", "A private place for your books". The app records less about authorship than a pen did — the ledger shows "Payment received · OP-1004 · Cash" with no author and no time, only a date. None of the fixes require inventing a sharing model.

- App.tsx:1216 — demo numbering collides after a void and presents contiguous numbers, which PRODUCT.md says the real product cannot promise.
- App.tsx:258 — go() resets search and filter on every navigation.
- App.tsx:433 — the nav count shows all invoices including voided. The owner cares about unpaid.
- Print filename: window.print() names the PDF from the title tag, so the customer receives "Opervia · Your business, in balance.pdf" rather than "INV-1042.pdf".
- InvoicePrint.tsx:39 — the blank sheet prints a hand-writable number field with no warning that hand-numbered sheets will collide with the Postgres sequence.
- styles.css:1 — render-blocking third-party Google Fonts import on every load. Self-host and subset.
- styles.css:629 — .badge.unpaid is blue-grey while every other status sits in the warm family.
- Empty-state copy is poetry where instruction is needed: "A clean page", "Meet your first customer", "Nothing spent, nothing missed".
- Auth.tsx:135 — the recovery screen hides the email field, so password managers have no username to associate the new password with.

## Questions to Consider

1. The carbon copy was the paper book's killer feature. Why does Opervia start from an empty grid every time?
2. The printed sheet is the product — so why is home a KPI dashboard instead of the book itself?
3. A receivables ledger exists to answer one question: who do I call today? Opervia never asks it.
